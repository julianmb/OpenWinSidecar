# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to each other. That's it.

## Expected

- Assume the other person is competent and acting in good faith.
- Criticise the code, not the person. "This will deadlock" is useful; "this is careless" is not.
- Accept that people have different hardware, different priorities and different amounts of time.
- Say when you don't know. A wrong confident answer costs more than "I'm not sure, let me check".

## Not accepted

- Harassment, personal attacks, or sustained disruption.
- Bad-faith argument about licensing, nationality, or how much someone's machine cost.
- Publishing someone's private information, or screenshots of their screen without permission.
- Expecting support on your timeline. I am one person; there is no SLA.

## Scope

Applies in issues, pull requests, discussions, and anywhere someone is representing the project.

## Enforcement

Report it to the maintainer by email or via
[GitHub's private advisory reporting](https://github.com/julianmb/OpenWinSidecar/security/advisories/new).
Reports are handled confidentially.

I will usually start with a conversation. If conduct continues, I may remove comments, close
issues, or block a contributor. I would rather be slightly too lenient than drive away someone
who reported a real bug badly.

## Note on project decisions

I make the final calls on architecture and releases, and I will often disagree with you. That
is fine — argue your case, and if I still say no, that's the end of it. Disagreeing with a
maintainer's decision about their project is not a code of conduct matter.
